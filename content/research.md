#### [Single- and double-headed odd radio circles in the LOFAR surveys](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202658988)
<p class="paper-ref">De Gasperin et al. 2026, A&amp;A 709, A154</p>

Odd radio circles (ORCs) are rare, ring-shaped radio sources about an arcminute across, and their origin is still debated. Searching the LOFAR Two-metre Sky Survey (DR3) at 144 MHz, we found 18 sources with ORC structure: four double-ringed ORCs (two of them new), five new single ORCs and nine candidates. Most sit around large elliptical galaxies, and larger ORCs tend to have steeper radio spectra.

<article class="paper" markdown="1">
#### [The Cosmic Ray Life Cycle in Galaxy Clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26005)
<p class="paper-ref">De Gasperin et al. 2026, Advancing Astrophysics with the SKA</p>

Turbulence and shocks in galaxy clusters heat the gas, amplify magnetic fields and accelerate cosmic-ray electrons, which shine as radio halos, relics and other cluster-wide sources. This chapter argues that the SKA should combine deep observations of selected nearby clusters with wide, shallower surveys: its sensitivity at low frequencies and in polarisation makes it ideal to trace magnetic fields and the old, low-energy cosmic rays that survive for billions of years.

<article class="paper" markdown="1">
#### [The ViCTORIA project: a multi-frequency radio survey of the Virgo galaxy cluster](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202452060)
<p class="paper-ref">De Gasperin et al. 2025, A&amp;A 693, A189</p>

The Virgo cluster is the nearest rich galaxy cluster and is still forming, but its size and the bright radio source M 87 have made it hard to image well. ViCTORIA combines LOFAR (LBA and HBA) and MeerKAT observations from 42 MHz to 1.7 GHz, about 60 times deeper than earlier data, together with polarisation and a blind H I survey. First results include the sharpest images yet of the lobes around M 87, which turn out to be full of filaments.
